Welcome to 3D Interactive Media Development

Overview:

This module introduces the development of interactive 3D prototypes and the core concepts of 3D User Interfaces.

Collaborative Teamwork:

You will work in groups of 4-5 students, integrating strengths from Digital Media, Computer Science, and Games Development.

The Goal:

Moving beyond simple "games" to better understand 3D User Interface concepts, and to create Interactive Dioramas that inform and educate the user.

Deliverable:

A finished interactive 3D prototype for your professional portfolio.

Proforma Overview

This module introduces students to concepts of 3D user interfaces and 3D animated and interactive media. It covers essential topics, including 3D modelling principles, methods and techniques, complex 3D objects creation, deformation and transformations, texture mapping methods, colour and lighting. It uses an industry standard games engine to demonstrate 3D animation concepts, properties, controllers and scripting to create 3D animated, interactive media content.

Learning Outcomes
  • LO1: Demonstrate an applied understanding of 3D modelling concepts and the underlying geometrical principles and apply appropriate techniques and tools to create 3D interactive media.
  • LO2: Incorporate 3D content in an industry standard games platform/engine.
  • LO3: Demonstrate an applied understanding of 3D animation concept, properties, controllers and use of scripting techniques to create in 3D animation.
  • LO4: Demonstrate an applied understanding of scripting techniques to incorporate interactive behaviours on 3D concept.
  • LO5: Work individually, as well as an effective team member to develop a common 3D interactive media product.
  • LO6: Communicate design concepts by oral and visual means and provide documentation for a 3D interactive media product in written form.
Module Aims
  • L5-M-LO1-MC - Specify, design, develop and test IMPs to solve real-life medium-scale problems with appropriate techniques.
  • L5-M-LO5-CS - Demonstrate competency in object-oriented design and algorithmic and mathematical approaches to solve medium scale problems.
  • L5-P-LO3-MC - Produce assets for the development of an IMP through appropriate professional digital content creation, following an object oriented approach.
  • L5-P-LO8-CS - Utilise, compare and contrast software frameworks and architectures and implement solutions using object-oriented programming.
  • L5-D-LO6-MC - Employ a standard process such as storyboarding to design, represent and formally communicate the specification of an IMP.
  • L5-D-LO4-CS - Employ a standard design language for the design, representation and formal specification of software.
  • L5-O-LO7-MC - Explain the life cycle of IMPs in the constraints of its operating technical environment.
  • L5-O-LO7-CS - Explain the basic principles of modern operating systems and how they serve operational needs.
  • L5-S-LO11-MC - Demonstrate professional responsibility in the development of quality interactive media solutions in a global context and the presentation and defence of these in multiple communication forms, supported by methodical research.
  • L5-S-LO10-CS - Demonstrate professional responsibility in the development of quality computer science solutions in a global context and the presentation and defence of these in multiple communication forms, supported by methodical research.

Coursework (Group work)

Note: Groups will consist of equal numbers of CS, DM, CG, and other students. This has worked well in the past to play to each others strengths and also facilitate knowledge transfer.

Special Note: This means your groups will not be in your tutorial sessions. This is normal, and does not affect your ability to plan and work together. Tutorials are for individual development. Group work is to be done outside of tutorials as part of independent study. All proformas indicate independent study hours are required to complete the module outside of normal lecture and tutorial time.

Lecture: 24 hours

Tutorial: 24 hours

Module and course based general study: 104 hours

Preparation for assessment: 48 hours

Total outside of class: 152 hours (this is what we look for in marking)

Assessment criteria

The student will demonstrate achievement of the learning outcomes by being able to:

  • Compose a 3D interactive media product following given technical requirements without restricting creativity.
  • Use appropriate tools and scripting language to create 3D animated, interactive media content and deploy it for multiple platforms and devices.
  • Demonstrate professional behaviour in role identification, delegation, and quality-assured team development.
  • Communicate design concepts by oral and visual means.
  • Write documentation of a 3D interactive media product.

Assessment criteria (higher marks)

To achieve higher marks, students must demonstrate a greater degree of expertise and knowledge, and in particular demonstrate knowledge of economic programming skills, creative skills, and the ability to effectively work in a group.

Marking
  • 70 or above: Exceptional effort, meeting all requirements and demonstrating work beyond topics covered in class.
  • 60-70: Considerable effort, meeting all requirements, but not going beyond class topics.
  • 50-60: Some effort, meeting most requirements, or meeting requirements with limited understanding.
  • 40-50: Limited effort, meeting only a few requirements, or with very poor understanding.
  • Below 40: Fail - not adequately attempted, with insufficient understanding.

Syllabus:

3D User Interfaces (UIs) and interaction styles:

introduce applications of 3D UIs, video gaming, large displays, mobile applications, etc. Discuss principles of designing Usable 3D UIs. Understand the design space.

3D world representation and 3D space transformation

Key concept of 3D modelling, primitives:

polygonal mesh representation (vertices, edges, faces), creating and editing a scene from primitives, polygon editing, parametric curves, NURBs and spline-based modelling concepts, lathing, extruding and Boolean modelling techniques to create complex models groups and hierarchies, construction history.

3D Interactive Media Development

Syllabus:

Understanding Visual effects, shading (composing and colour grading), texturing mapping (UV texture layout, coordinates, mapping) and paint effects. Brief introduction to lighting (illumination model, light types and properties) and shading (mental ray shading).

Preparation for animation, basic skeletons (not full body character) and basic rigging techniques (not to the extent of character animation).

Animation techniques, timeline based animation; motion paths; basic relationships: constraints, set driven keys. Standard 3D UI for 3D interactive games engines.

Interactivity, control a 3D geometry and animation properties with the use of appropriate scripting language.

Virtual Environment – 3DUI Needed

At its most basic, we need some sort of 3D UI to interact with a Virtual Environment.

We may think we know instinctively what a VE is, but to correctly define and use 3D UI concepts, we must know what flavour of VE we are using or need.

This is complicated greatly by public perceptions of VEs and the misnaming and misconceptions that happens.
