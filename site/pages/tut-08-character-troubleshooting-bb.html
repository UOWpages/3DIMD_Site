  3. Play the two scenes: Kinematic RigidBody Controllers 01 and Dynamic RigidBody Controllers 01.

What each script shows

  • physicsController_rbAF - AddForce, world space. Physics pushes you around; mass matters.
  • physicsController_rbTAF - AddForce along local axes. Same forces, object-relative direction.
  • physicsController_rbMP - Rigidbody.MovePosition (IJKL). Direct but still collides along the path.
  • physicsController_rbMPT - MovePosition + rotation (arrow keys). Drive-style control.
  • KinematicWASDController - isKinematic = true. You control everything; physics pushes nothing.
  • KinematicWaypointMover / KinematicFollow / KinematicRotate - script-driven platforms and movers.

Concepts to spot

  • Physics code lives in FixedUpdate, not Update.
  • AddForce = momentum and drift. MovePosition = precise, still collision-aware.
  • isKinematic flips who is in charge: the physics engine or your script.
  • World space vs local (transform) space changes what "forward" means.

Tasks

Task 1: Feel the mass
  1. Open Dynamic RigidBody Controllers 01.
  2. Select the character using physicsController_rbAF.
  3. Play with Rigidbody Mass = 1, then again with Mass = 10.

Check: heavier = slower to accelerate and slower to stop. AddForce with ForceMode.Force is mass-dependent.

Task 2: Swap the ForceMode
  1. Open physicsController_rbAF.cs.
  2. Change ForceMode.Force to ForceMode.VelocityChange, then try ForceMode.Acceleration.
  3. Play and compare the feel of each.

Check: VelocityChange and Acceleration ignore mass - the response is instant and identical at any mass.

Task 3: Crash test - AddForce vs MovePosition
  1. Drive the rbAF character (WASD) into a wall or obstacle.
  2. Drive the rbMP character (IJKL) into the same obstacle.
  3. Watch what each does on impact and when you keep pushing.

Check: the AddForce character carries momentum and can bounce or slide; MovePosition stops cleanly but still respects the collision.

Task 4: Flip isKinematic
  1. Open Kinematic RigidBody Controllers 01 and select the KinematicWASDController character.
  2. Play and push it into a dynamic object, then let a moving object hit it.
  3. Untick Is Kinematic on its Rigidbody while playing and repeat.

Check: kinematic = immovable and pushes others; dynamic = gets pushed back. isKinematic decides who is in charge - your script or the physics engine.

Task 5: World vs local forward
  1. Rotate the rbAF character 90° on Y in the Inspector, then press W.
  2. Do the same with the rbTAF character.

Check: rbAF still moves in world forward (ignores the rotation); rbTAF moves where the object faces. World space vs local space changes what "forward" means.

Task 6 (stretch): Build your own waypoint platform
  1. Create a cube, add a Rigidbody, and add KinematicWaypointMover.
  2. Create 2-3 empty GameObjects as waypoints and assign them to the list.
  3. Play, then try standing a character on the platform as it moves.

Check: the platform patrols via MovePosition - but the character slides off. Fixing that is the Moving Platforms with Characters section below.
