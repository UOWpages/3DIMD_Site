Unity Lighting and Shaders — Unity 6.3

Lecture Overview

Unity 6000.3.19f1 • Universal Render Pipeline (URP). This lecture explains how lights illuminate a scene, how physically based materials respond, and how to choose efficient lighting for interactive 3D applications.

Learning outcomes: distinguish direct and indirect light; configure Realtime, Baked and Mixed lights; use URP Lit materials; understand lightmaps and probes; identify mobile/VR performance considerations.

Check Smoothness on Imported Models

Distinguish mesh smoothing (vertex normals and tangents) from material smoothness (surface reflection response). In the Model Import Settings, inspect Normals and Tangents. In a URP Lit material, adjust Smoothness separately.

Three Light Modes — Not Three Types of GI
  • Realtime: evaluates direct lighting dynamically.
  • Baked: stores precomputed illumination for static geometry in lightmaps.
  • Mixed: combines realtime and baked contributions, depending on the selected mixed-lighting mode.

Global illumination (GI) describes indirect light bouncing between surfaces; it is a separate concept from a Light component's Mode.

Realtime Lighting in a Scene

Direct Lighting

Direct lighting travels from a light source to a surface without an intermediate bounce. A realtime light can react to movement, rotation, intensity and colour changes. Indirect fill can still come from ambient/environment lighting or previously baked data.

Shadows are not inherently black: ambient and indirect contributions can illuminate shadowed surfaces.

Light Component Types
  • Directional: parallel rays; orientation matters, position generally does not.
  • Point: emits in all directions from a position.
  • Spot: emits into a cone.
  • Area: provides baked lighting in standard URP workflows; not a general-purpose realtime area-light substitute.
Other Illumination Sources

Environment/ambient lighting, skyboxes, emissive surfaces, Light Probes and Reflection Probes complement explicit Light components. Emission makes a surface appear luminous but does not automatically illuminate surrounding geometry in realtime.

Baked Lighting and Global Illumination

Indirect Illumination

GI models light reflected from one surface onto another. Typical visible effects include colour bleeding and indirect fill. Soft shadows and bounced illumination are related visual phenomena but are not interchangeable.

Baked Global Illumination

The lightmapper calculates illumination ahead of runtime and stores the result in lightmaps for suitable static geometry. Baked lighting is efficient at runtime, but a baked lightmap does not update when a light or object moves.

Progressive Lightmapping

In Unity 6.3, teach the Progressive Lightmapper workflow for baked GI. Select an appropriate lightmapper in Lighting Settings, mark eligible Mesh Renderers as contributing to GI, ensure suitable lightmap UVs, then generate lighting.

Global Illumination Concepts and Setup

Direct vs Indirect Lighting

Direct: light travels from source to surface. Indirect: light has bounced from one or more surfaces. PBR governs material/light interaction; GI governs how indirect light is represented or approximated.

Lightmaps, Light Probes and Reflection Probes
  • Lightmaps: baked lighting on static geometry.
  • Light Probes: sampled indirect illumination for objects not using baked lightmaps.
  • Reflection Probes: environment reflections for reflective materials.
Practical URP Lighting Workflow

Set up a URP scene; add a directional light and environment lighting; decide which objects are static; configure Realtime/Baked/Mixed lights; enable baked GI as required; generate lighting; evaluate quality and frame time.

Ambient Light and Skyboxes

Ambient/Environment Lighting

Environment lighting supplies broad illumination even when no direct light reaches a surface. The ambient source can be derived from the skybox or configured independently. Distinguish ambient diffuse illumination from environment reflections.

Skybox Materials

Assign a compatible skybox material in the Lighting window. Skybox appearance, ambient lighting and reflection updates are related but not identical settings.

Reflection Probes

Reflection Probes provide local environment reflections. Their capture mode and update frequency have performance implications.

Light Quality and Rendering Cost

Light Rendering in URP

Legacy per-light Important / Not Important rendering modes belong to older Built-in Render Pipeline teaching. In URP, configure the Main Light and Additional Lights through the URP Asset and renderer settings.

Per-Pixel and Per-Vertex Lighting

Per-pixel evaluation usually offers more accurate results than per-vertex evaluation but can cost more. URP may provide additional-light evaluation options depending on configuration and renderer path.

Shadows

Shadow resolution, cascade count, shadow distance and the number of shadow-casting lights affect image quality and GPU workload. Test in the actual target build.

Lighting and Material Properties

How Materials Respond to Light

URP Lit combines base colour, metallic/specular response, smoothness, normal mapping, occlusion and emission to produce physically based shading.

Emission and GI

An emissive material appears bright. Its emission can contribute to baked lighting when configured for GI, but it is not automatically a realtime light source.

Normal Maps and Ambient Occlusion

Normal maps modify apparent surface orientation without adding geometry. Ambient occlusion approximates local occlusion; it does not replace full GI.

URP Lit and Physically Based Rendering

URP Lit Replaces Standard Shader as Primary Example

For URP projects, use Universal Render Pipeline/Lit as the principal PBR shader. The Built-in Render Pipeline Standard Shader is legacy/comparative material, not the default URP shader.

Core URP Lit Inputs

  • Metallic or Specular workflow
  • Smoothness
  • Normal Map and Occlusion Map
  • Emission

Metallic and Specular Workflows

Metallic Workflow

The metallic workflow describes whether a surface behaves like a dielectric or a metal, with a smoothness value controlling the breadth of reflections. Metallic and smoothness are separate material properties.

Specular Workflow

The specular workflow allows direct specification of specular reflectance. It is an alternative authoring workflow, not another name for metalness.

PBR Consistency

Use realistic albedo/base colours and appropriate metallic values. Compare the same material under multiple lighting environments to avoid overfitting to one scene.

URP Lit Maps and Surface Options

Texture Maps

Base Map supplies surface colour; Normal Map simulates fine detail; Metallic/Specular maps control reflectance; Occlusion Map supplies local occlusion; Emission Map specifies emissive regions.

Opaque and Transparent Surfaces

Use Surface Type and associated blending/alpha options in URP Lit rather than legacy Standard Shader Rendering Mode labels. Transparency can increase rendering cost and sorting complexity.

URP Fundamentals

What Is URP?

The Universal Render Pipeline is Unity's Scriptable Render Pipeline for scalable graphics across a range of hardware. A URP project uses URP assets, renderer settings and compatible shaders.

Built-in vs URP

Built-in Standard materials and certain legacy shader features are not directly interchangeable with URP Lit. A scene may require material conversion and visual validation.

Migrating Materials to URP

Before Conversion

Back up or commit the project. Confirm URP is installed and configured. Inventory custom shaders and third-party materials; automatic conversion cannot guarantee support for custom shaders.

Conversion Workflow

Use Unity's available render-pipeline conversion tools for the installed Editor/URP version. Inspect the menu in your specific project rather than following a Unity 5-era menu path. Verify converted materials individually.

After Conversion

Check surface appearance, transparency, normal maps, emission, lighting and shadows. Rework incompatible shaders in Shader Graph or URP-compatible HLSL where needed.

URP Shaders, Shadows and VR Performance

URP Lit and Shader Graph

Use URP Lit for standard PBR surfaces. Use Shader Graph to construct custom visual effects without writing all shader code manually. Confirm the shader targets URP.

Lighting for Mobile and Standalone VR

Prefer baked illumination for largely static scenes; minimise realtime shadow casters and unnecessary additional lights; use Light Probes where appropriate; profile on the target headset, not only in the Editor.

Final Checklist
  • Project uses the intended URP asset and renderer.
  • Lights use deliberate Realtime/Baked/Mixed modes.
  • GI participation and lightmap UVs are correct.
  • Materials use URP-compatible shaders.
  • Shadows and additional lights are profiled on target hardware.
